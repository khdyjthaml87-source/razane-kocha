the most famous places

Algiers Casbah

The Casbah of Algiers is one of Algeria’s most important historical and cultural landmarks and a remarkable example of a traditional Mediterranean Muslim city. Located on the hills overlooking the Mediterranean Sea, the Casbah is characterized by its narrow winding streets, whitewashed houses, traditional courtyards, historic mosques, Ottoman palaces, old markets, and ancient urban structures. Its unique architecture reflects centuries of interaction between Mediterranean, Islamic, Ottoman, and North African traditions.

The Great Mosque of Algiers – Djamaa El Djazair

The Great Mosque of Algiers, known as Djamaa El Djazair, is one of the most remarkable modern architectural landmarks in Algeria. Located on the Mediterranean coast in the eastern part of Algiers, the mosque combines elements of traditional Islamic and Maghrebi architecture with contemporary engineering and design. Its monumental complex includes a vast prayer hall, courtyards, landscaped spaces, and a striking minaret that dominates the surrounding skyline.

The Hamma Test Garden – Jardin d’Essai du Hamma

The Hamma Test Garden, also known as Jardin d’Essai du Hamma, is one of the most famous botanical gardens in Algeria and one of the most beautiful green spaces in Algiers. Located in the Hamma district near the Mediterranean coast, the garden provides a peaceful contrast to the busy urban environment of the capital. It contains a remarkable variety of trees, plants, flowers, and landscaped areas, making it an important destination for nature lovers, families, photographers, and visitors interested in botany

Sidi M’Cid Bridge – Constantine

Sidi M’Cid Bridge is one of the most iconic landmarks of Constantine, a city famously known as the “City of Bridges.” The bridge spans the spectacular Rhummel Gorge and connects the historic parts of Constantine across the deep ravine. Its dramatic position above the canyon makes it one of the most impressive viewpoints in the city, offering visitors striking views of the surrounding cliffs, valleys, and urban landscape

Timgad

Timgad is one of Algeria’s most important archaeological sites and one of the finest surviving examples of Roman urban planning in North Africa. Located on the northern slopes of the Aurès Mountains in Batna Province, the ancient city was founded by the Roman Emperor Trajan around AD 100 as a military colony. Its carefully planned streets, organized according to the Roman cardo and decumanus, create a remarkably clear grid that can still be seen today

The Djurdjura Mountains

The Djurdjura Mountains are one of the most spectacular mountainous regions of northern Algeria and an important natural landmark of the Kabylie region. Forming part of the Tell Atlas mountain system, Djurdjura is characterized by dramatic peaks, steep cliffs, deep valleys, forests, and high-altitude landscapes. The region offers a striking combination of rugged mountain scenery and rich natural environments, making it an attractive destination for hiking, nature exploration, photography, and outdoor activities
